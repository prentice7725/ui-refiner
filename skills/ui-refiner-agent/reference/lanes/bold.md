# Bold

The direction with one hand behind it from the first screen to the footer: the whole page is built in a single **material**, a finished look taken from a physical thing (an enamel sign, a tiled wall, a till receipt). In the round-18 pilot the material pages scored highest for distinctiveness, and they were the ones judges were most split on: memorable when the material suited the client, a gimmick when it did not.

Read [README.md](README.md) first, then [the materials README](../materials/README.md), then the dealt material's file.

## What is dealt

- A hero structure that suits the material (photo-led for a material that needs photographs, without one for a material that rarely uses them).
- One material, and one alternate.

## Choosing between the dealt material and the alternate

Use the dealt material unless it clearly does not suit this client: the thing it is taken from has nothing to do with what the client does, or its ground fights the subject (in the pilot an instrument-panel look on a household money app was ranked last by every judge). Then use the alternate, and say in one line why. Do not pick a third.

## How to build it

- **Build every surface and control the way the file says.** A page that only borrows the material's colours is not in the material. Take the tokens as written; only a real brand colour replaces the accent, and a real brand typeface the faces.
- **Follow the file's Rhythm section.** The material's own surfaces go at full strength in a few places (the first screen, and one or two others), and the rest of the page rests on a plain ground. A surface repeated in every section reads as wallpaper: in the pilot a hiring page that turned every section into a receipt was called memorable and monotonous in the same sentence.
- **The headline and the main action stay inside the first screen.** A photograph or a surface never pushes them below the fold.
- **The signature element is made in the material**, from the page's real content.
- **Keep out what the file lists under Never**, even when the page had it before.
- A gradient, a shadow or a tinted ground that the file calls for is deliberate. Say so when the scan reports it.
