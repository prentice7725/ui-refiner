# Changelog

All notable changes to UI Refiner. Dates are in Asia/Tokyo. Versions follow `package.json`; the deep-gate report `version` field and comparison contexts carry the same number.

## Unreleased

- The theme gallery is in English (`index.html`), Korean (`index.ko.html`) and Japanese (`index.ja.html`), each linking to the others; the note on a published sample links back to the gallery in the sample's language.

## 0.14.0 — 2026-11-26

Applying a theme now keeps what is the client's own, and the theme gallery with its 14 samples is published on GitHub Pages.

### Added
- Applying a theme now starts with what is the client's own: before the theme goes on, the skill lists up to six traits of the original in `ui-refiner-traits.json` (always asking what the first photograph shows, what the page leads with and which device it stages), builds around them, and afterwards has each marked kept, weakened or lost, repairing the weight-3 ones. On nine real themed pages (24 November 2026, three passes by two judges) content and grouping survived while the clients' own visual devices (0 of 8 kept) and lead order (0 of 3) were flattened into the theme's standard blocks. With the step, three of those pages kept 86–100% of their weighted traits against 47–64% without it (25 November 2026, one run each).
- The theme gallery and the 14 samples are published on GitHub Pages (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`, built by `scripts/build-pages.mjs`). Each published sample carries a small note that the business is invented, with a link back to the gallery, and the gallery says so under its title. Three sample businesses shared their name and trade with real companies and are renamed: 酒井酒造 → 汐見酒造 (`shop-portal-story`), 한빛정밀 → 새움정밀 (`business-dark`), 한라농원 → 귤담농원 (`shop-portal-grid`); their screenshots and README thumbnails are redrawn. The Korean business archetype demo (`docs/archetype-demo`) uses 새움정밀 too.
- Themes README, "Between themes of one family": for each family with more than one theme, what in the client's content tips the choice (for example `public-portal-search` when visitors know the name of what they want, `public-portal-purpose` when they arrive with a situation). The skill decides between such themes by that content and no longer by where a theme was measured.

### Changed
- Own traits, tightened after a quality judging (25 November 2026): a kept trait is redrawn in the theme's colours, type and shapes, not carried over as it stands; the first screen keeps one lead; an urgent notice outranks every trait; what is not on the list is tidied as usual. With the first wording, blind designers would ship the page built without the step in 10 of 12 comparisons, while a judge playing the client's designer chose the page built with it in 7 of 11. With the tightened wording, on the same three pages: blind designers preferred the page built with the step in 7 of 11 (all of them on two pages, none on the third), the client's-designer judge in 12 of 12, and 69–100% of the weighted traits still survived. A further line limiting the hero to one kept device held on one page; one replacing banner walls with text links was withdrawn after it erased the traits those banners carried, and now says to order the banners and keep the ones with a character of their own as images (untested).
- A client with no logo file gets its name as a wordmark in the page's typeface, with no symbol drawn beside it; the marks in the theme samples are stand-ins and come out of client pages (untested).
- `/ui-refine` no longer tells the agent to pick a theme by country.

## 0.13.0 — 2026-11-23

Themes are no longer tied to a country, and a theme can be started in a project with the client's own colour and language. A theme's name now says its family and look; the country is only where it was measured. `/ui-refine` starts Korean and Japanese trade pages from a theme.

### Added
- `ui-refiner theme list` and `ui-refiner theme starter <theme> [--brand "#rrggbb"] [--face "Typeface"]`: copies a theme's `theme.css` and `theme.md` into a project and writes `brand.css`, which sets the client's colour and typeface on the theme's tokens (the soft and deep tokens keep the theme's lightness steps with the client's hue), and `STARTER.md`, which lists the photographs the theme needs with their place and ratio. With `--brand` it renders the theme's sample in that colour and lists each text or button whose contrast the colour broke at 1440 and 390px, with the nearest shade that passes (a warning: the files are written and the command exits 0). No sample page or generated image is copied.
- New theme `business-corporate-quickbar` (Korean logistics, makers, builders; standard corporate, 41 measured sites): full-width photograph hero, a quick bar for tracking and quotes, business tiles, figures band, sites table, notices and customer centre. A logistics page on `business-white` had read as a software template.
- Optional Modern Web Guidance: when the person asks for it or the project has it installed, the skill reads its guides before writing HTML, CSS or client-side JS. It decides how things are built, not how the page looks, and is off by default (no measurable difference on three pages, 19 November 2026). UI Refiner does not bundle or install it.
- `scripts/refine-bench`: theme benchmark arms (`o`, `p`), `theme-judge.mjs`, and `FIT_FILE` for `trade-fit.mjs`.

### Changed
- **Themes renamed: the country prefix is gone.** A theme's name now carries its family and look only; where two themes share both, a trait tells them apart. `kr-care-white` → `care-white`, `jp-care-corporate` → `care-corporate`, `kr-business-dark` → `business-dark`, `kr-business-white` → `business-white`, `kr-creative-dark` → `creative-dark`, `jp-creative-bigtype` → `creative-bigtype`, `kr-place-photo` → `place-photo-view`, `jp-place-photo` → `place-photo-quiet`, `kr-public-portal` → `public-portal-search`, `jp-public-portal` → `public-portal-purpose`, `kr-shop-portal` → `shop-portal-grid`, `jp-shop-portal` → `shop-portal-story`, `kr-business-corporate` → `business-corporate-quickbar`, `jp-business-corporate` → `business-corporate-message`. Links to the old folder names no longer resolve. The gallery and the README grid group themes by family, and the gallery's country filter is removed.
- A theme is no longer tied to the country it was measured in. The themes README and the skill choose by purpose family first, and say that the country is the theme's source, not a limit on its use. `ui-refiner theme starter --lang ko|ja` moves a theme to the other language: the font stacks (Latin faces kept, serif for serif), line height (±0.15, the measured gap between the Korean and Japanese themes) and line breaking, and lists what to check by hand.
- `/ui-refine` starts a Korean or Japanese trade page with no brand of its own from a theme instead of three directions; the directions stay for other pages and on request. On six trade pages from `scripts/refine-bench/trades.tsv` (one run each, a blind judge asked in both orders) the themed page read as the more real site on all six once the fixes below were in, the judge would ship it on five with one split, and it cost $11.3 against $33.0. Only the two pages the theme first lost were run again after the fixes.
- Taking a theme, the skill fetches credited stand-in photographs first when the client has none, and never carries a theme sample's generated images onto a client's page. A dental page built on the sample's empty generated rooms had lost to one with stand-ins of a dentist at work.
- The READMEs explain how the themes were built (eight page types, six trade families, the themes, one family drawn in all eight types) and show the themes as a linked grid. CI checks that the README grid and the demonstration pages are up to date with their sources.

The archetype pipeline behind the themes is in the repository, and the theme images take a third of the space.

### Changed

- `local/` is git-ignored, for raw scans and experiment folders kept next to the checkout.

## 0.10.0 — 2026-11-20

The design now leads. Models build pages well enough that telling AI-made from human-made pages matters less; what still goes wrong is that their pages look alike and not like the trade they are for.

### Added
- Themes: 13 finished designs measured from 1761 human-made Korean and Japanese sites (`skills/ui-refiner-agent/reference/themes/`).
  - **How they were measured.** Each site's stored scan and desktop screenshot gave 23 features: the share of the first screens that is white, dark, photograph or saturated colour, type sizes, content width, page length, hairlines and more. k-means (k = 8) grouped them into eight impressions. The trades were grouped into six purpose families: place, care, public, shop, business, creative.
  - **One theme per family and country.** Each is the most common impression of its family and country:
    - place: `kr-place-photo`, `jp-place-photo`
    - care: `kr-care-white`, `jp-care-corporate`
    - public: `kr-public-portal`, `jp-public-portal`
    - shop: `kr-shop-portal`, `jp-shop-portal`
    - business: `kr-business-dark`, `kr-business-white`, `jp-business-corporate`
    - creative: `kr-creative-dark`, `jp-creative-colour`
  - **What each holds.**
    - `theme.md`: the measured basis (p25, median, p75), tokens, the section skeleton, components, rules and things to avoid.
    - `theme.css` and a sample page for a fictional client, with desktop and phone screenshots. Every sample passes `stress`.
    - Generated photographs from Grok Imagine and Codex, with their prompts in `prompts.json`. They are marked `data-generated` and named as samples in the footer. `generated.check.mjs` checks that every listed image is present at its size.
  - **The themes README** says how to pick a theme and how to apply it to a client's content.
    - Photo-led and dark themes need the client's own photographs. On a logistics page without any, the dark theme read as a text template.
    - Keep the client's menu and drop sections without content. Never invent news, figures or quotes.
    - Applied to three real pages (a Japanese inn, a Korean dental clinic, a Korean logistics firm), every link, form, field and figure was kept and `stress` passed.
  - **Limits.** The two creative themes rest on 12 and 7 sites. Some samples are shorter than their measured page length.
- The skill starts a new Korean or Japanese site without its own design system from a theme ("Build a new screen"). Its description names the themes, so a request such as "업종 테마로 만들어 줘" or "テーマで作って" brings it in.
- The three READMEs open with the themes and a grid of their sample pages (`docs/assets/themes.jpg`); refine, memory, checks and the scan follow under "What else you get". The guides' quick start points to the themes.
- The scan checks three rules from Vercel's Web Interface Guidelines as quality items (`src/interface-checks.mjs`): a viewport meta that blocks zoom (`zoom-disabled`: `user-scalable=no` or `maximum-scale` under 2), a text field under 16px on the phone viewport, which makes iOS Safari zoom the page on focus (`input-zoom`), and a field whose `onpaste` refuses paste (`paste-blocked`). On 60 human-made Korean and Japanese sites from the genre list, 13 blocked zoom, 9 had small fields and none refused paste, so none of them is scored. The collector runs without eval, so a page's content security policy cannot stop it. `...` against `…` from the same guidelines is left out: it is a house style on many Korean and Japanese sites.
- The scan lists placeholder text left in the page as a quality item (`placeholder-text`, `src/placeholders.mjs`): an all-zero phone number or postcode, "○○町" or "OO구" beside an address part, "Lorem ipsum", a "[Company Name]" slot, an `@example.com` address. It found 35 of 79 Korean and Japanese pages that Claude and Codex built from one-line prompts and 2 of 1845 human-made Korean and Japanese sites (20 November 2026; "〇〇な方へ" and board tags such as "[회사소식]" are left alone). It is not scored: the values come from a brief without facts, not from how a model writes. The refine report lists the ones left for the client to supply. The same measurement tried the second-generation copy tells from taste-skill (em dashes, "not X but Y", negation runs, virtue words, personas, two-fragment lines) on these pages: none separated AI-built from human-made Korean and Japanese pages, and none was added.

### Changed
- AI-look index: on Korean and Japanese pages a cream ground with a serif display (`paper-serif`) scores as strong (3) instead of moderate (2). It was on 44–50% of 79 Korean and Japanese pages that Claude and Codex built from one-line prompts and on 0.5–1.5% of 1790 human-made Korean and Japanese sites. Rescanned on 20 November 2026, score ≥ 20 flags 73% of those AI pages (was 65%; Claude 81%, Codex 64%; 12 held-out prompts 58% → 67%), while human-made sites stay at 7% in Korea and go from 2.6% to 2.9% in Japan (three sites that scored 19). English pages keep it moderate. Removing the deliberate-design discount was tried and rejected: real photographs and a chosen web font are still far more common on human sites (75–88% against 29–35%), and without the discount human Korean sites at 20 or more would go from 7% to 11%.

## 0.9.0 — 2026-11-20

### Added
- The three READMEs open with the refine, then what the toolkit remembers (`prefer`, the log of directions not picked) and the checks (`stress`, the loading checks in `directions verify`). They report the first refine run with this release on three Korean and Japanese pages, and move the trade benchmark and the 0.7.0 eight-page result into one short paragraph on what does not help yet. The guides' quick start lists `stress` and `prefer`.
- `scripts/calibration-ai-krjp.tsv`: 36 Korean and Japanese one-line prompts for an AI-built calibration sample. Built once each by Claude Opus 5.5 and Codex (gpt-6.2-sol, user-installed skills hidden), plus 7 Claude-built benchmark pages, 79 pages in all: score ≥ 20 flags 65% of them (Korean 72%, Japanese 58%; Claude 72%, Codex 56%). The README and the guides now give these figures instead of saying the Korean and Japanese catch rate is unmeasured. Scoring `accent-word` and `tinted-page` on Korean and Japanese pages was tested on 12 held-out prompts and not adopted (one more page caught in 24, about one point more human sites at 20 or more).
- `directions record --said "<the person's words>"` keeps what the person said about the choice, word for word (newlines collapsed, at most 500 characters), and every record now keeps the directions shown but not picked (`rejected`: label, lane, hero, faces, ground, accent, material). Only the pick feeds later deals; the rejected looks were never shipped, so they are not counted as repeats across clients. `prefer suggest` reads both.
- `ui-refiner stress <page|url>`: a worst-case data check, after Emil Kowalski's break-ui skill. It swaps realistic Korean, Japanese or English values into the rendered text at 320, 390 and 1440px (longer labels on buttons and at most two menu links, a second clause after each heading's last line, long emails, URLs and phone numbers with an extension, big figures; `.example` domains only), and lists only what the swap broke: text off screen or clipped, text lying on another element, the page scrolling sideways (broken), a label breaking onto more lines or an image changing shape (ugly), each with the CSS that usually fixes it. It writes `STRESS.md`, `stress.json` and a screenshot per kind and width that broke, and exits 1 when something broke. Logo links, links inside sentences, menu toggles and fixed or sticky bars are left out. On the 19 November 2026 Korean dental page it found a phone badge, positioned absolutely, that grows over the opening-hours card at 320px; on three refined mocks of that page, the header phone number running off the screen at 320–390px. The refine runs it after `keep check`, and building a new screen runs it after the scan.
- `ui-refiner prefer add|remove|list|suggest`: the person's taste as things the deal leaves out, in `preferences.json` next to the refine log. Axes are hero structure, face, accent family, tailored surface (brand, dark, paper) and material; an entry can be limited to one page kind (`--kind`) and needs the person's own words (`--said`). `directions deal` leaves the applying entries out of all three directions as far as each pool allows (an avoided surface also rules out materials on that ground), adds avoided faces to `avoidFaces`, and lists them in `DEAL.json` `preferences` and in its output. Only "avoid", no "favour", so one person's taste does not pull every client to one look. Entries are never deleted: adding the same one again marks the old one replaced, `remove` marks it retired. `suggest` lists recorded words not yet used and heroes, materials and display faces shown in three or more rejected directions and never picked, for the agent to ask about; it writes nothing.
- `directions deal --seed <n>` fixes the draw, so two runs with one seed differ only in their inputs.
- Refine benchmark harness: a trade benchmark. Only stage 1 has been run (see `scripts/refine-bench/README.md`): naming the trade did not bring the familiar mock closer to the trade's measured sites. Arms k/l build only the familiar mock (A) without and with `--trade`, on one seed per page and repeat; arms m/n refine to the end and pick A. Six Korean and Japanese pages are listed in `scripts/refine-bench/trades.tsv` (ko-logistics is the control), with prompts for three new ones. `scripts/refine-bench/trade-fit.mjs` measures how well each mock fits the trade's measured sites. See `scripts/refine-bench/README.md`.

- Refine benchmark harness: arm w runs the three-direction refine with Chrome's Modern Web Guidance skill (`variants/mwg.md`; `EXTRA_DIR` hands the agent extra files), and `scripts/refine-bench/mwg-audit.mjs` measures axe violations, Lighthouse accessibility, best practices and performance, MWG `legacy-scan` findings and the AI-look score. One run each on ja-recruit, ja-clinic and ko-logistics (19 November 2026, $35.09): the agent searched MWG 15–19 times a page, but both arms ended with no serious axe violations, accessibility 99.7 and best practices 96. The guidance is not added to the refine.

### Changed
- `directions verify` (and so the finish gate) fails when a web-font stylesheet blocks the first paint (a plain `<link rel="stylesheet">` or `@import` to Google Fonts, Bunny, Typekit or a Pretendard CDN) or when a stand-in photo has no `srcset` (`src/page-loading.mjs`). All six refined benchmark pages loaded Google Fonts that way, with `preconnect` and `display=swap` already in place. Loading the same stylesheet with `media="print" onload="this.media='all'"` and a `<noscript>` copy took ja-recruit's desktop Lighthouse performance from 70 to 100 (largest paint 2.9 s → 0.66 s) and ja-clinic's from 60 to 68 (4.1 s → 2.6 s), with no added layout shift. The skill says how to load fonts and to ask only for the weights the page uses, since each Japanese or Korean weight is fetched as dozens of slices.
- `ui-refiner photos` saves each stand-in as WebP at 640, 1024 and 1600 px with the bundled Chromium and prints a ready `<img>` line with `srcset`, `width` and `height`; the skill tells the agent to set `sizes`, `fetchpriority` and `loading` from it. A 1600 px Commons JPEG weighed 430–610 KB; the 1600 px WebP weighs 130–230 KB and the 640 px one 24–42 KB. On the refined benchmark pages the total weight fell by 120–630 KB, and on a throttled phone ja-clinic's largest paint came 2.9 s sooner (11.3 s → 8.4 s). The Lighthouse performance score barely moved, because render-blocking web fonts (350 KB–1 MB a page) hold the first paint. `--no-optimise` keeps the download as it is.
- AI-look index: stock marketing phrases (`buzzword-copy`) and a display under twice the body (`flat-scale`) are reported but no longer scored. A recheck on 19 November 2026 rescanned the calibration lists (145 AI-built, 110 English-language human sites) and rescored 1790 human-made Korean and Japanese sites: stock phrases showed up on 13% of AI sites and 14% of English human ones, a flat scale on 8% and 19%. Without them, score ≥ 20 flags 69–74% of AI sites (one of 145 lost), 0–9% of English human sites (was 0–15%), 7% of Korean and 3% of Japanese ones. Korean false positives come from Korean commercial style (dense banners, pop-ups, card grids, gradients, emoji, glass), which the strong signals also see on AI sites, so they were left as they are. All AI-built samples are English; the catch rate on AI-built Korean and Japanese pages is still unmeasured. The calibration tables in the guides and the README line now show these figures.
- `directions deal`: the "images first" structure (`below-fold`) asks for images across the full width with the claim on them or in a short band right under them, never in a column beside a half-width photo. In the first run of the trade benchmark, 4 of 13 mocks dealt it were built as a photo on one half and the headline on the other, which is a different structure (`left+media`, `right+media`).
- The first-screen colour measure moved from `scripts/collect-colours.mjs` to `src/first-screen-colour.mjs`, so the benchmark measures mocks the same way the collector measures sites. The collector's output is unchanged.

### Fixed
- `ui-refiner stress` reported an overlap when a changed button sat in a fixed or sticky bar (a phone and booking bar at the bottom of a phone screen) and the page text scrolling under the bar crossed it. Such an element is now compared only with what shares its bar. In the first refine run with the check (ja-clinic, 19 November 2026), this false overlap was the only broken row on the first run; the agent's change to the bar then clipped both buttons at 320 and 390px, which it fixed on the next run.
- `ui-refiner photos` wrote the `<img>` and `srcset` paths relative to the folder the command ran in, so an agent running it from the UI Refiner checkout got `../../Users/…` paths that break in the page. They now start from the project folder that holds `assets/` (`<project>/assets/stand-in` → `assets/stand-in/…`), or from the page's folder with `--page <html file>`, and the output says which folder that is.
- The trade benchmark scores a mock's hero structure by family, as the deal does. A mock dealt "images first" (`below-fold`) keeps its claim on the first screen as the lane asks, so it measures as a full-bleed photo; in the first trial run this read as the agent drifting from its deal.
- The first-screen colour measure missed a photo under a text overlay. It walked up from the topmost element only, so a photo beside its overlay in the tree, or one darkened by a `::after` overlay over a fallback colour, read as white or as that colour. It now looks through every element stacked at each point, and it no longer fails on pages without a body element. Both colour surveys were measured again on 19 November 2026: `data/colour-reference` now covers 552 sites and reads 52% of first screens as photos (before: 548 sites, 35%), and `data/genre-reference/colour` covers 1942 sites with photos on 65% (before: 46%). In the trade groups the photo share rose most for Korean games (13% → 60%), Japanese farms (29% → 75%) and Japanese makers (52% → 85%); Japanese inns went from 64% to 79%. `academy/KR` regained its colour shares, so all 72 groups report them.

## 0.8.0 — 2026-11-18

### Added
- Korean pages: a display size under twice the body (`flat-scale`) and a single plain typeface (`generic-type`) are reported without being scored, as on Japanese pages. On the 180 human-made Korean sites in `data/kr-reference` they were found on 43% and 29%, and with both scored 49% of the sites reached 8 points or more; without them, 33% do (9% reach 20). These shares are re-scored from the signals recorded per site; `data/kr-reference/SUMMARY.md` still shows the figures under the earlier rule until the set is collected again.
- The verdict for 8–19 points reads "unclear" instead of "some traces" (`aiLook.verdict: "unclear"`). A fifth of the Japanese and about a quarter of the Korean human-made reference sites score in that range. The report's basis line now gives the Korean figures beside the Japanese ones.
- `ui-refiner scan` also writes `scan-graded.png`: the desktop screenshot marked up like a graded paper. Each scored signal is circled in red where it was found (up to three places per signal) with its points beside the first, and the score and verdict are written at the top.
